01 / About

Building reliable systems from hardware to firmware

I'm a Biomedical Engineering (Hons.) graduate from Bannari Amman Institute of Technology, with hands-on experience building embedded hardware and firmware systems at Qneuro India.

I write ARM Cortex-M firmware for real-time EEG acquisition.

Most of what I build sits at the same junction: a physical sensor on one side — an accelerometer, an ECG front-end, a pressure array — and a microcontroller on the other that has to sample it, process it correctly, and get the result somewhere useful without falling behind in real time.

That constraint is what I enjoy. Whether it's fitting a QRS detector into a 2 ms sampling budget on an STM32F401, or getting three identical I²C accelerometers to talk to one ESP32 without address conflicts, the interesting part is almost always the boundary between the hardware's limits and what the signal actually needs.

STM32F401RE

Real-Time ECG Streaming Pipeline

Firmware · signal processing
View repository
PROBLEMDetecting heartbeats reliably on a microcontroller means processing every sample within a fixed 2 ms budget, without ever buffering a full recording.
APPROACHSampled ECG at 500 Hz on a TIM3-triggered ADC, ran it through a 4th-order IIR band-pass filter (5–20 Hz, Direct Form II Transposed), then a Pan-Tompkins-style QRS detector — derivative, squaring, moving-window integration, adaptive threshold. Heart rate is the median of the last 5 RR intervals, gated to a 30–220 BPM plausible range, alongside a weighted 0–100 signal-quality score. Results stream out over a compact UART protocol with an XOR checksum.
STACK
STM32F401RECIIR filteringPan-Tompkins QRS detectionUART protocol design
RESULTConstant per-sample (O(1)) processing with bounded memory, plus a host-side C simulator that validates the same algorithm on a PC — against a Python analysis layer — before it ever touches hardware.
